**[00:00] Priya:** Thanks for coming back, Dana. Last week we covered how orders arrive. Today I want to stay on the price: what happens to it between the order landing and it being final. I will ask some open questions first and then get specific. Can you walk me through the last chain order you personally looked at?

**[00:40] Dana:** Sure. Thursday, FreshMart, their weekly EDI order. About 1,200 cases across maybe forty lines. It lands in the system, the order desk checks nothing is missing, and the price is already there. Every line comes in at list minus their negotiated price, and the chain discount is on top of that.

**[01:20] Priya:** Who touches the price on an order like that?

**Dana:** Nobody, for a standard order. The order desk does not change prices, they are not allowed to. It is only when a rep is pushing something, a new flavour or a shelf reset, that they add a discount on top. On the FreshMart one nobody touched it.

**[01:55] Priya:** Roughly how many orders a week, and how many get a manual discount?

**Dana:** Chains and distributors together, maybe three hundred orders a week. Manual discounts, I would guess ten or fifteen of those. It is not most orders. Marketplace is separate, that is hundreds of small orders and nobody touches those.

**[02:30] Priya:** Let us build the price up from list. A chain, say FreshMart. What do they pay?

**Dana:** List price minus their chain discount. All the chains get eight percent off list, automatically. The rep does not do anything, it is just on the account. Then on top of that whatever we have negotiated on specific products, which is most of the range for FreshMart honestly.

**[03:10] Priya:** Eight percent for every chain, always?

**Dana:** Every chain, yes. Distributors are different, they buy on a different price list, no percentage. Marketplace is its own price list too.

**[03:35] Priya:** The negotiated prices per product. Who maintains those?

**Dana:** The key account manager agrees them with the buyer, sends them to the order desk, and the order desk keys them in.

**[04:30] Priya:** Now manual discounts. A rep wants to give FreshMart a bit extra on a new flavour to get it on shelf. How much can they give on their own?

**Dana:** The rep can knock off up to ten percent on their own. Anything above ten comes to me. I get a notification, I look at it, I approve or I do not. That is the rule and the reps know it.

**[05:00] Priya:** Up to ten without approval, above ten needs you. How does the approval reach you?

**Dana:** It shows up in my inbox in the system, the worklist thing. Sometimes the rep also messages me because the worklist is slow to refresh. I approve maybe two or three a week.

**[05:30] Priya:** Two or three a week, out of ten or fifteen manual discounts. So most manual discounts do not come to you?

**Dana:** Most are under ten, yes. Reps stay under ten if they can, they do not like waiting on me.

**[05:50] Tom:** Is there an upper limit at all? Could a rep type in thirty percent and send it to you?

**Dana:** They should not be able to. I think the system stops them somewhere, but I could not tell you the number. Nobody has tried thirty.

**[06:15] Priya:** When did the ten percent rule come in, and why?

**Dana:** 2019, I think. It used to be looser and we bled margin on a couple of listings, so finance and my predecessor tightened it. I inherited it.

**[06:40] Priya:** Promotions. Northgate runs a summer promo on the Citrus line, twelve percent off for six weeks. If they also place a big order that qualifies for the volume discount, do they get both?

**Dana:** No. One or the other. We never stack a promo on top of the volume discount, the promo replaces it. That is a rule from finance, it has been like that as long as I have been here.

**[07:10] Priya:** And how is that enforced? Does the system take one off, or does the rep?

**Dana:** I assume the system does it. The reps do not touch it. I have never had to check.

**[07:30] Priya:** OK, I will verify that. Any buy-X-get-Y offers, free cases, samples?

**Dana:** Samples go through marketing on a separate order type, that is not sales. Free cases, no, we do not do that on chain orders. Not that I know of.

**[07:55] Priya:** Anything that changes the price or the quantity on a line that nobody enters by hand?

**Dana:** [pause] There is one thing that is odd, now that you say quantity. Every so often the warehouse tells me an order shipped with more cases than we quoted. Like the customer ordered fifty cases of Classic Cola and fifty-five went out. The rep says they did not add anything, the warehouse says the system had it on the pick list. It happens maybe once a month. I have never got to the bottom of it.

**[08:35] Priya:** Which products, do you remember?

**Dana:** Always the Classic range, I think. Never the new stuff. And the customer never complains, so it drops off the list.

**[08:55] Priya:** That is useful, I will dig into it. Marketplace orders, do reps ever discount those?

**Dana:** No. Marketplace is fixed price. The price is the price, we do not negotiate with an algorithm. The reps cannot even see those orders most of the time.

**[09:20] Priya:** Let us do pain points. What goes wrong most often with pricing?

**Dana:** Two things. The worklist, as I said, it is slow and reps message me anyway. And chargebacks. A chain short-pays because they think a promo price should have applied and we think it should not, and it takes AR weeks to untangle. That is not really my process but it lands on me.

**[10:00] Priya:** Any pricing that happens outside the system? Spreadsheets, emails, credit notes after the fact?

**Dana:** The KAMs keep their own spreadsheets of what they agreed with each buyer. The order desk works off those. If the two disagree, the spreadsheet wins and the order desk fixes the system. I know that is not ideal.

**[10:30] Priya:** Anything the system does that nobody can explain?

**Dana:** The extra cases thing. And occasionally a price on a line is different from what the KAM agreed and nobody knows why. Usually an old number.

**[10:50] Priya:** Who else should I talk to about pricing?

**Dana:** Marco in finance, he owns the discount policy. Lena on the order desk, she keys everything. And marketing for promos, that is Sam.

**[11:10] Priya:** Last one. If you could change one thing about pricing?

**Dana:** I want to see every discount over ten percent before it ships, and I want the KAM spreadsheets gone. If the system had the agreed price, the order desk would not need to fix anything.
